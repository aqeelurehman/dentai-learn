Odontogenic Cysts
Comprehensive overview of common odontogenic cysts and their clinical significance in oral pathology.
Radicular Cyst: Most common jaw cyst arising from periapical granuloma epithelium
Dentigerous Cyst: Arises around crown of unerupted tooth
Radicular Cyst: Figure — Radicular cyst
Dentigerous Cyst: Comprehensive centrated oral nonorm cyst
Definition
Odontogenic cysts are pathological cavities lined by epithelium derived from odontogenic tissue — fluid-filled, bone-enclosed, with diverse clinical behavior.
Key Features
• Well-defined radiolucency · • Epithelial lining · • Fluid-filled cavity

Ameloblastoma
Definition: A benign but locally aggressive odontogenic tumor arising from remnants of dental lamina. Most common in posterior mandible (70–80%).
Symptoms:
Painless jaw swelling
Odontogenic cyst-like expansion
Diagnostic diasprosis pattern
Diagnosis:
Histopathology showing follicular/plexiform pattern with reversed nuclear polarity.

Ameloblastoma — Key Symptoms
The distinctive feature of Odontogenic Keratocyst (OKC) is its parakeratinised lining and high recurrence due to satellite cysts.

Ameloblastoma's unique features: follicular pattern with reversed polarity of peripheral cells. Locally aggressive — requires wide resection.

Key Takeaway: PKC lining is parakeratinized.
📋 Pathology Summary
Ameloblastoma — locally aggressive benign odontogenic neoplasm. Follicular or plexiform histology. Wide resection treatment preferred.
